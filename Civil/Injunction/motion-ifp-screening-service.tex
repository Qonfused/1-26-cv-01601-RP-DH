\section*{Pre-Service Review and Resulting Service}

Section 1915(a)(1) governs financial eligibility to proceed without prepayment. Section 1915(e)(2) requires dismissal when the Court determines that a listed ground applies. Rule 72 directs a magistrate judge to conduct assigned nondispositive and dispositive proceedings promptly. Plaintiff requests prompt completion since the remaining Fall 2026 enrollment and course-add periods are now measured in days, and delays in service and briefing could prevent effective relief.

The pending pre-service process can be completed through a ruling on Dkt. 3, the disposition resulting from § 1915(e)(2) review, and, for each defendant against whom at least one claim may proceed, a service order. The service order may direct the Clerk to issue the corresponding summons and direct the Marshal to serve the then-operative complaint.

The referral procedures also address any proposed claim dismissal. A magistrate judge may enter orders on nondispositive matters under § 636(b)(1)(A). A claim-dispositive screening result may be presented to the district judge through proposed findings and recommendations under § 636(b)(1)(B) and Rule 72(b). The screening disposition may authorize service on some defendants, recommend dismissal of other claims or defendants, or do both.

\section*{Remaining Time for Fall 2026 Relief}

Plaintiff's preliminary-injunction request concerns Fall 2026 accommodations, enrollment, course planning, and prerequisite decisions. The proposed relief includes administrative enrollment and deadline adjustments when ordinary registration dates have passed. Dkt. 8 at 1--2.

UT's current Fall 2026 schedule resumes registration on August 19 and begins classes on August 24. Ordinary class-add periods close August 27 for graduate and law students and August 31 for undergraduate students. Late-registration and permission-based add procedures remain available through September 9. University of Texas at Austin, Office of the Registrar, \textit{2026--2027 Academic Calendar}; \textit{When to Register and Pay, Fall 2026} (last visited Aug. 6, 2026). Those later procedures leave a limited opportunity for administrative relief, but they do not eliminate the time required for summons issuance, service, renewed Rule 65 briefing, judicial consideration, and University implementation.

After IFP review and screening, several steps remain before Plaintiff can obtain effective Fall 2026 relief. For each defendant authorized for service, the Clerk must issue the corresponding summons, and the Marshal must serve the summons and then-operative complaint. Plaintiff must then refile the preliminary-injunction motion as permitted by the July 7 order. Defendants must receive an opportunity to respond. The Court must then decide the motion, and any Defendant against whom relief is granted must comply with the Court's order.

Prompt completion of pre-service review can preserve the remaining possibility of effective Fall 2026 relief, including late or administrative enrollment, course registration, and implementation of prospective accommodations. If the Court requires additional financial information, corrected summonses or other process, clarification of the pleading, an amended complaint, or a hearing before completing review, Plaintiff requests that the Court identify or schedule the required step promptly and set any necessary response deadline.
